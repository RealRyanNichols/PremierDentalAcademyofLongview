Three systems, one tooth

Universal on most US charts, FDI on anything international, Palmer on orthodontic and oral-surgery notes. You will read all three. Type any of them in and see the other two.

Convert a tooth

Type a Universal number (1–32 or A–T), an FDI code (like 46), or Palmer notation (like LR6).

The trap worth knowing

Some numbers are valid in both systems and mean different teeth. 14 is the upper left first molar in Universal, and the upper right first premolar in FDI. When a chart just says "14", the system it is written in is the whole question — so this tool always tells you which one it read your input as.

How each system counts

Universal

One continuous run of 1–32. Starts at the upper right third molar, goes left across the top to 16, drops to the lower left third molar at 17, and comes back to 32 at the lower right. Baby teeth are A–T on the same path.

FDI (ISO 3950)

Two digits. The first is the quadrant — 1 upper right, 2 upper left, 3 lower left, 4 lower right — and the second is how far out from the midline the tooth sits. Baby teeth use quadrants 5–8.

Palmer

A quadrant bracket plus 1–8 counting out from the midline. Written with an angled symbol on paper; here it is shown as UR, UL, LL or LR so it can be typed. Baby teeth use A–E.

The thing that makes Universal awkward to convert is that it runs midline-outward in two quadrants and outward-to-midline in the other two, while Palmer and FDI always count out from the midline. That is why 8 and 9 are neighbours either side of the front, but 1 and 32 are the back teeth on opposite sides.
