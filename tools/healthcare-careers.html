Healthcare careers with a short road in

Ten jobs you can train for in under two years, side by side. Real figures from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — including the ones that pay more than dental assisting, because you deserve to see the whole picture before you pick.

How to read these numbers

A median is the midpoint: half the people doing that job earned more than the figure, half earned less. It is a national number, not a starting wage, and not what any particular employer in East Texas will offer you. Treat it as a way to compare jobs against each other, which is what it is good for.

Pay figures are median annual wages from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2024. Growth figures are from U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Employment Projections, 2024–2034. For comparison, the median annual wage across all occupations was $49,500 and average projected growth was 3%. Figures last checked 2026-08-12.

Where dental assisting sits in that list

Two of the ten pay more at the median — surgical technologist and licensed vocational nurse — and both take considerably longer to train for. Dental assisting sits in the upper half of the group on pay with one of the shorter training paths, which is the trade-off worth understanding before you commit either way.
