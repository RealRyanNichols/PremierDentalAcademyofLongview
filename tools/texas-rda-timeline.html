From nothing to registered

Every step to becoming a Registered Dental Assistant in Texas, in the order you actually do them — with what to have ready at each one, and who makes the decision. Print it and tick it off.

Verify at the source before you pay for anything

Requirements, fees and processing times are set by TSBDE and change. Confirm the current details at tsbde.texas.gov before you pay for anything. Requirements are set by the Texas State Board of Dental Examiners (TSBDE) and were checked on 2026-08-12.

You will notice there are no fees or processing times on this page. That is deliberate — those are exactly the details that change, and they are exactly the details somebody would budget around. Get the current figures from tsbde.texas.gov.

  1. 1

    High school diploma or GED

    The baseline TSBDE asks for before you can register as a dental assistant.

    What to have ready: Your diploma or GED certificate · A photo ID that matches the name on it

    Not finished yet? Free adult-education and GED classes run across East Texas — dial 211 or ask Workforce Solutions. This does not have to stop you starting.

    Who decides: Your school district or GED testing centre issues it.

  2. 2

    Complete a TSBDE-approved registration course This part is us

    The training programme itself. This is the part Premier Dental Academy does — about 12 weeks in person at the Longview campus, or self-paced online as distance education.

    What to have ready: Enrolment paperwork · Your tuition arrangement — $3,000 paid in full or $3,500 on a plan

    A school certificate and state registration are two different things. Finishing here proves you completed training; the state credential comes from TSBDE and no school can hand it to you.

    Who decides: Your course provider confirms completion. TSBDE decides whether the course is approved.

  3. 3

    Hands-on BLS / CPR certification

    A current basic life support course. It has to be the hands-on kind.

    What to have ready: Your BLS or CPR card, in date

    A course delivered entirely online is not accepted. Book the version where you physically practise compressions with an instructor.

    Who decides: The training organisation that runs the course (American Heart Association, Red Cross and similar).

  4. 4

    Pass the registration examination

    The exam covers three areas: jurisprudence, infection control and radiology.

    What to have ready: Whatever ID and confirmation the testing provider asks for

    This is the exam our practice questions and drills are built around. Work the per-topic report rather than re-sitting the whole thing each time.

    Who decides: TSBDE sets the exam; the approved provider administers it.

  5. 5

    Dental Assistant Radiology Certificate

    A separate TSBDE credential. You need it to legally position or expose dental x-rays in Texas.

    What to have ready: Proof you completed the radiology requirement

    People assume registration covers x-rays. It is its own certificate, and taking radiographs without it is not something an office can let you do.

    Who decides: TSBDE issues the certificate.

  6. 6

    Texas Jurisprudence Assessment — if your course was over a year ago

    If more than a year has passed between your course exam and your application, TSBDE asks for the Jurisprudence Assessment from its website, or a current DANB CDA card.

    What to have ready: Your jurisprudence assessment result, or your current DANB CDA card

    This is the step that catches people who trained, took a break, and came back to it. If you finished a while ago, check this before you apply rather than after.

    Who decides: TSBDE.

  7. 7

    Apply to TSBDE for registration

    Submit the application with your supporting documents and the fee TSBDE is charging at the time.

    What to have ready: Course completion proof · BLS/CPR card · Exam results · Radiology certificate · Photo ID · The application fee

    We deliberately do not print the fee or a processing time here — both are set by TSBDE and both change. Check the current figures on their site before you budget.

    Who decides: TSBDE. This is the milestone that lets you work as an RDA in Texas.

  8. 8

    Keep it current

    RDA registration renews on a two-year cycle, with continuing education required each period. Human trafficking prevention training is also required for renewal.

    What to have ready: Your CE certificates from approved providers

    Twelve CE hours every two years with a board-approved provider is the figure TSBDE has published. Verify the current requirement at renewal time — this is the kind of detail that changes.

    Who decides: TSBDE sets the renewal cycle and the CE requirement.

The one distinction worth getting straight

A certificate is what a school gives you for finishing its programme. That is us. Registration is what the state gives you, and it comes from TSBDE. Finishing training and being registered are two separate milestones, and no school anywhere can hand you the state's credential. If somebody tells you otherwise, that is a reason to walk away.
