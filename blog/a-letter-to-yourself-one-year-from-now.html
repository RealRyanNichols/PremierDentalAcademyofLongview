Write a letter to yourself one year from now — here's how

Here's an exercise that costs fifteen minutes and a stamp (or a scheduled email), and lands harder than most planning tools ever will: write a letter to the person you'll be one year from today. Not a goals list — a letter. Goals lists talk AT the future; letters make the future a witness.

Why this works when planning apps don't

A letter to next-year-you does three things a goals list can't. It forces specificity — you can't write 'be better' to a real reader, even a future one. It creates accountability without an audience — nobody else ever has to see it, but SOMEONE is now waiting. And it captures today's truth — a year from now, the letter shows you exactly how far you came, in your own handwriting, which no app screenshot has ever done for anyone.

Three ways to deliver mail to the future

Paper: sealed envelope, 'open on [date]' on the front, somewhere you'll find it (sock drawer, Bible, toolbox). Email: most email apps can schedule a send a year out — write it, schedule it, forget it. Phone: a note plus a calendar reminder for the date. The medium doesn't matter; the date does.

If your letter mentions a career change

Notice what you wrote in prompt four — the 'refuse to still be doing' one. If a job or a stuck-ness showed up there, your letter just handed you a deadline: one year to make that sentence false. Twelve-week credentials exist for exactly this shape of deadline (the map is here, ours is free to visit) — and next-year-you, opening the envelope, will know whether you meant it. No pressure.
