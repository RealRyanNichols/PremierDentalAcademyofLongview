How to renew your Texas RDA registration (before it lapses)

Almost nobody lets their registration lapse on purpose. It happens because the renewal notice went to an old email, or a move swallowed the mail, or a busy season swallowed the calendar. The fix isn't working harder — it's a small system you set up once and then stop thinking about.

First, be clear about what you're renewing

Two different things get mixed up constantly, and the difference matters on the day something goes wrong.

Your certificate of completion is what a school gives you. Ours says you finished our program. It doesn't expire, and it isn't a state credential. Your RDA registration is what the Texas State Board of Dental Examiners issues, and that's the one that has to stay current for you to keep working as a registered dental assistant in Texas. If you're still sorting out how the two fit together, the long version is in our guide to Texas RDA registration.

The one thing we can't do for you

Premier Dental Academy does not renew, file, submit, or track anybody's registration — not for current students, not for graduates, not for people who finished with us years ago. No school does. Renewal is between you and the Board. If anyone ever tells you their school "handles that part," they're confused about how this works, and that's not something you want to discover the hard way.

Why it lapses on good, careful people

Around East Texas offices the reasons repeat themselves, and not one of them is "she didn't care":

  • The email is old. The address on file is a work account from two jobs ago, or a provider you stopped checking. Notices go out; nobody reads them.
  • The address is old. You moved across town, mail forwarding expired, and the envelope went to somebody else's kitchen counter.
  • Life got loud. A baby, a move, a sick parent. The year disappeared and took the reminder with it.
  • You stepped away and came back. Time out of the operatory is the single most common way people lose the thread.
  • You assumed the office handles it. Plenty of offices are wonderful about reminding their team. A reminder is a kindness, not a transfer of responsibility.

Set the calendar once, and make it loud

Put it on the calendar you actually live in — the one on your phone, the one that buzzes at you. Not a sticky note on the fridge and not "I'll remember." Make it a yearly repeating event so you never have to set it again.

Then add the thing most people skip: a second reminder a few weeks ahead of the first, labeled with the actual tasks. "Renewal" is a word you can ignore. "Log in, check CE, update address, renew" is a short list you can knock out in one sitting at the kitchen table.

Keep your CE certificates somewhere you can find them at 9 p.m.

Continuing education is where the scramble usually happens — not because people skip the courses, but because the proof ends up scattered across three inboxes, a glovebox, and a folder somebody's spouse helpfully cleaned out.

Pick one place and use it every single time: a folder in your cloud drive, or an album in your phone's photos. The moment a course ends, photograph or download the certificate and drop it in. Name it so it sorts — year first, then the topic. It takes about eleven seconds and it saves you an entire evening later. If you took a course through us, save your own copy the same way rather than counting on anyone else's records.

A good habit: update before you change anything

Before you move, change employers, change your name, or switch email addresses — log in and update your contact information with the Board first, while you're already thinking about it. Nearly every lapse story we hear starts with a notice that went somewhere the person no longer reads.

If you think it may have already lapsed

Don't guess, and don't keep working on an assumption. Log in and look at your own record, or contact the Board directly and ask plainly what your status is. If there is a problem, tell your employer before they find it themselves — an assistant who caught it and is already fixing it is in a completely different position from one who didn't know.

What we won't do here is tell you what happens next, what anything costs, or how long you have to fix it. Those rules belong to the Board, they change, and a blog post confidently quoting last year's answer is worse than no answer at all.

Where every specific actually lives

Renewal periods, fees, continuing-education requirements, late and reinstatement rules, and the online renewal system itself are all on the Texas State Board of Dental Examiners site: tsbde.texas.gov. That's the authority. Check it there every time rather than trusting a number you read somewhere — including here.

Not registered yet? Different problem, easier one

If you landed here while researching the career rather than maintaining it, renewal is a long way off. The first step is training. Our Longview program runs about twelve weeks with classes capped at eight students, and there's a fully online path covering the same curriculum if driving to campus isn't realistic. Have a look at what the program covers, compare it against the online option, or work out the money side with the tuition planner before you talk to anybody.

And if you're already working and want to add to what you hold, coronal polishing certification in Texas is the next step people ask us about most.
