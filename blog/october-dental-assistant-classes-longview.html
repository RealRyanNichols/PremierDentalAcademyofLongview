Which start fits which kind of life

Here is the honest sorting, based on who does well in each room.

October 5 (Mon/Wed/Fri mornings) tends to fit people with school-age kids, people working afternoon or evening shifts, and people who learn better in shorter, more frequent doses. Three mornings a week means the material never sits cold for long. The trade: your week has class in it three times, so childcare and transportation have to work three days instead of two.

October 20 (Tue/Thu full days) tends to fit people who drive in from out of town, people whose employer can carve out two fixed days, and people who would rather protect three untouched weekdays. The trade: a six-hour class day is a real day, and if you are stacking a shift on top of it, you will feel it by week three.

The question that decides it: not "which schedule do I like better?" but "which one can I still do in week nine, on a week when something goes wrong?" Pick for your worst week, not your best one.

What the weeks between now and then should look like

You have a few weeks. Most people spend them waiting. The ones who start strong spend them getting ready — and none of this costs anything.

Learn the vocabulary before day one and the first two weeks stop feeling like a foreign language. Mesial, distal, occlusal, prophy, BOP — you will hear all of it in the first hour. Work the flashcards ten minutes a day and you will walk in ahead.

Sort the logistics that quietly sink people: who watches the kids on class days, which car you are driving, what your employer knows. Handle those now, while it is a conversation instead of an emergency.

If neither October date works

Then do not force it. November 9 (Mon/Wed/Fri) and November 17 (Tue/Thu) are both on the books, and the calendar stays current.

Or start from home. The online program is self-paced and begins any day — same curriculum, no class-day schedule to build your week around. It is coursework, not hands-on chair time, and we will always tell you that plainly.
