What slow actually is

Slow is your brain doing the work consciously that will later run automatically. Right now, passing an instrument takes attention: grip, angle, timing, all deliberate. With repetitions, those steps compress into one motion you stop thinking about — that compression is skill, and there is no path to it that skips the deliberate, clumsy phase. The classmate who looks faster isn't more talented; they're a few dozen reps ahead, or they're rushing and hiding errors, which is worse.

Why your school should expect you to be slow

This is the real reason we cap classes at eight. In a class of forty, the slow week goes unnoticed until it becomes a failing grade. In a class of eight, the instructor sees the fumble the day it happens, walks over, and fixes the grip — which converts your slowest weeks into your fastest learning. Being watched while you're bad at something feels awful and is exactly what you're paying for.

The comparison trap

Measure against yourself last week — never against the person at the next chair. "Faster than Tuesday-me" is the only scoreboard that predicts anything about your career.

What we will not tell you

We won't invent a story about a struggling student who became a star, and we won't quote a made-up statistic about how many people feel this way. What we can tell you plainly: the fumbling phase is built into how hands learn, our whole program is designed around watching you through it, and quitting during it is the only version of this that actually ends badly. If the feeling is bigger than clumsiness — if you're genuinely spiraling — read what to do when you feel behind and then say something to your instructor. Out loud. This week.

A note for the people who haven't started yet

If you're reading this while deciding whether to enroll: yes, you will be slow at first, here like anywhere. The difference worth shopping for is whether anyone will be close enough to notice and correct you while it happens. That's the eight-seat answer — come see the room and picture yourself fumbling safely in it.
