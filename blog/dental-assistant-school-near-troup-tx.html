Name the real question first

Troup sits right on the Smith and Cherokee county line, south of Tyler, and like most towns its size it does not have a dental assisting program of its own. East Texas is not short on people who would be good at this work. It is short on places to learn it within ten minutes of home.

So the honest framing is not where is the school in Troup. It is: given where you live and what your week looks like, which of these three do you pick?

  • Drive to an in-person program. Longview and Tyler are both realistic directions from Troup.
  • Train online from your kitchen table. Self-paced, start any day.
  • Wait — not forever, but until one specific thing changes.

All three are legitimate choices. Two of them are wrong for you, and which two depends entirely on your life, not on your motivation. Here is each one with the downside left in.

Option one: drive it

Our campus is at 2800 Gilmer Road in Longview. If you are coming from Troup, the natural run is north — Troup to Overton to Kilgore and on into Longview — and it is roughly an hour, give or take, depending on which end of town you start from and which road you like. Do not take our number for it. Put your own address into a map app and look at the trip on a weekday morning at the hour class would start, because that is the drive you would actually be making.

What the drive buys you is the part that does not translate to a screen: real instruments in your hands, somebody standing beside you to correct your grip the first time it is wrong instead of the fiftieth, and classmates working the same material on the same day. Our classes are deliberately small — small enough that nobody gets to sit quietly in the back and never touch anything.

Driving is wrong for you if: the vehicle is not reliable, the gas money is not there, or the only childcare you have is on the far side of that drive. None of those are motivation problems, and no amount of wanting it hard enough fixes them. Better to say it out loud now than to drop out in week five.

Class days are daytime — some cohorts run Mon/Wed/Fri and others Tue/Thu, depending on the start date you pick. Call or text (903) 913-6444 for the exact hours of the class you are looking at, so you are comparing a real schedule against your real week instead of guessing.

You are already on the road that leads here

Here is the small advantage of being where you are. Plenty of people in Troup already drive north for work, for shopping, for the kids' ball games. If that is you, the commute is not a new habit you have to build from nothing — it is an existing habit pointed at a different destination for twelve weeks.

That matters more than it sounds like it should. The commutes that break students are usually the ones that are a complete change to how the week runs. If you have driven that stretch of road a hundred times already, you know what it costs you, and you are far less likely to be surprised by it in week three.

We wrote more about the honest trade-offs of driving for dental work here: Longview to Tyler — is commuting for a dental job worth it? The same arithmetic applies to commuting for school.

Option two: train online from Troup

The online program is self-paced and starts any day you decide to start. That is the whole point of it: no waiting for a cohort to come around, no drive, and you can work it around a shift schedule that changes on you.

And here is the honest limit, which we would rather tell you than have you discover. Online teaches you the material. It does not put an instrument in your hand. If you pick it, plan from day one for where your hands-on practice is going to come from and how you intend to get comfortable in an operatory before somebody hires you into one. Online is not physical hands-on training and we will never describe it that way.

Online is wrong for you if: you already know you do not finish things without a room and a schedule holding you to it. Self-paced is freedom for some people and quicksand for others, and most of us know which one we are. Be honest with yourself before you pay for anything.

If you are genuinely torn, the long-form comparison is here: online vs. in-person dental assistant training.

Option three: wait — and when waiting is the right call

Nobody selling training wants to write the paragraph that tells you to wait, so most of them don't. We will.

That is not procrastination, that is sequencing — and starting a twelve-week program on top of an unresolved crisis is how good students end up quitting for reasons that had nothing to do with the material.

Waiting is the wrong call when you cannot name the thing. “I'm waiting until things calm down” is not a plan; things do not calm down, you just get older. If that is where you are, go pick a date off the class calendar and work backward from it.

Compare the same five things at every school

People shop programs on distance and price, then wonder why two schools that looked identical on paper turned out nothing alike. Ask every program you consider — including this one — the same five questions:

  1. How many students are in a class? This is the question that quietly decides how much you actually get to touch.
  2. Who is teaching, and are they in the room? Ask for a name.
  3. Can I come see it before I pay? If the answer is anything other than yes, that is your answer.
  4. Is the school licensed by the Texas Workforce Commission? We are — Career School License #S5316 — and you can look that up yourself rather than taking our word for it.
  5. Is the full cost written down, in one place, before I sign anything?

What it costs, and how people from small towns pay for it

Tuition, the payment-plan option and the exact installment math all live on the enrollment page and in the tuition planner, where you can build your own schedule and see real figures — rather than a number typed into a blog post that might be out of date by the time you read it.

And one thing worth straightening out early, because it confuses nearly everybody: finishing a program and being registered by the state are two separate events. We issue a Certificate of Completion when you finish. Becoming a Registered Dental Assistant in Texas is a separate process run by the Texas State Board of Dental Examiners, whose requirements are theirs to set and to change. For anything official — requirements, fees, deadlines, forms — go to the board directly, every time. Our Texas RDA registration guide explains how the pieces fit together.

Come look at the room before you decide

If you are an hour out, do not enroll in anything sight unseen. Come walk through the lab, meet whoever would be teaching you, and ask those five questions to our faces. A tour is free and takes less time than the drive there and back.
