The short answer: there is no program in Daingerfield

Let us get the disappointing part out of the way. There is no dental assisting program in Daingerfield. There is not one in Hughes Springs, and there is not one in Pittsburg either. Morris County is a good place to live and a thin place to go looking for career training. If you have already spent an evening typing things into a search bar, you know that.

What there is, south of you down 259, is a small school on Gilmer Road in Longview that does exactly one thing: train registered dental assistants. That is us — Premier Dental Academy of Longview, 2800 Gilmer Rd, Suite 106. We are licensed by the Texas Workforce Commission as a career school, license #S5316, and we have students who drive further than you would.

This article is not a sales pitch for the drive. It is the information we would want before deciding whether a drive like that is worth it, plus the option that skips the drive entirely.

What the drive from Morris County actually looks like

Daingerfield to our campus is roughly forty-five miles, almost all of it a straight shot south on US-259. Most people put that somewhere between forty-five minutes and an hour behind the wheel. Hughes Springs trims a few minutes off. Pittsburg lands in about the same place.

But roughly is doing real work in that sentence, so do yourself a favor before you decide anything: open a map, type in your actual address, and set the arrival time for the morning a class would start. That number is your number. Ours is an average, and nobody commutes on an average.

Three things people from up that way usually do not realize

It is not a five-day drive. In-person classes run in the daytime on either a Mon/Wed/Fri or a Tue/Thu schedule, depending on the cohort — so you are making the trip two or three mornings a week, not five.
It is one road. No stacked interchanges, no toll decisions, no urban merge. For a lot of people that matters more than the mileage.
Carpooling happens. Classes are small, people live all over East Texas, and riders find each other in the first week. We cannot promise you a seat in somebody else's truck, but it is worth asking.

Two ways to train from up there

There are genuinely two paths, and they are not the same product with a different price tag. One is a room with chairs, instruments and other people in it. The other is coursework you do at your kitchen table. Pick with your eyes open.

Tap through the three honest versions below, then read the one that sounds like your life.

What you actually get for the drive

If you are going to burn an hour each way, the training on the other end had better not be a slideshow.

The in-person program is built to feel like a working dental office rather than a classroom. Real equipment and real instruments, set up the way a chairside assistant would actually encounter them. On the software side, students train on the practice-management and clinical tools we built ourselves — a tooth chart you actually chart on, a six-point perio chart, a prescription pad that flags an allergy conflict before you can save it, a drag-and-drop schedule, SOAP-style clinical notes, and the claims and EOB workflow that trips up most new assistants in their first month.

You can poke at some of that from home right now, for free, before you ever get in the car. The Skills Lab and the free practice exam are open to anybody.

The other thing you get is a small room. Class sizes here are small on purpose — not as a marketing line but because charting, tray setup and four-handed work are things you have to do with your own hands, and that does not happen in a lecture hall. If you want the specifics of why that matters, four-handed dentistry is the clearest example.

The schedule question, answered honestly

Classes are daytime. That is the whole program now — there are no evening and no Saturday classes, and if you find an old page anywhere on the internet saying otherwise, it is out of date.

Exact hours vary by cohort, and we would rather you hear them from a person who can also tell you which cohort has room. Call or text (903) 913-6444 for exact hours. A real person at the school answers, not a call center, and it is a two-minute conversation.

If daytime does not work because of a job, read can you work while training as a dental assistant before you rule the whole thing out. Sometimes the answer is the online course. Sometimes it is a different cohort. Sometimes it is honestly not right now, and we will tell you that too.

The questions people from up that way actually ask

Do I need dental experience? No. No prior dental experience is required to start. Most people walk in having never held a mirror and explorer.

Is there anything closer? Not that we are aware of in Morris County — but do not take our word for it. We are an interested party. Search it yourself, and if you find something closer that fits you better, take it.

How long is this really? About twelve weeks for the in-person program. The online course is self-paced, so the honest answer is "as long as you take." The longer version is here.

Does finishing make me a registered dental assistant? No, and this one matters. Finishing here earns a Premier Dental Academy certificate of completion. Becoming a registered dental assistant in Texas is a separate step handled by the Texas State Board of Dental Examiners, with its own requirements. Anybody who blurs those two things together is doing you a disservice — the official source is tsbde.texas.gov, and our registration guide walks through how the pieces fit.
