A walkthrough of the free RDA practice exam tool

Before you sit for anything official in Texas, you should know exactly where you stand. Our free practice exam exists for one reason: to show you your soft spots while they're still cheap to fix. Here's how to use it well.

What this tool is — and what it isn't

The free RDA practice exam is a set of original practice questions built around the material Texas expects a Registered Dental Assistant to know — the same territory covered by the required radiology, jurisprudence, and infection control coursework. You answer multiple-choice questions, get a score, and see where you were shaky.

This is practice, not the state exam.

These are our own study questions — not official state exam questions, and a score here doesn't predict or guarantee anything official. For the real requirements, the authority is the Texas State Board of Dental Examiners. Think of our tool as the scrimmage before the game.

Starting your first practice run

Go to the practice exam page, unlock it with your name and email, and start answering. There's nothing to install and nothing to pay. It works fine on a phone, though if you have a laptop handy, the bigger screen makes it easier to read each question slowly — and reading slowly matters more than you'd think, because most missed questions are misread questions.

One piece of advice for the first run: don't study first. That sounds backwards, but the whole value of a first attempt is getting an honest baseline. If you cram the night before your own practice test, you've only measured your short-term memory. Take it cold, and the result tells you the truth.

What your score actually means

The number matters less than what's underneath it. A rough score on a cold first attempt is normal — most people researching dental assisting haven't studied radiation physics or Texas dental law yet, and there's no reason they'd score well. What you're looking for is the shape of your misses: were they clustered in one subject, or scattered everywhere? Clustered misses mean one focused study block can move your score a lot. Scattered misses usually mean vocabulary is the real problem — you can't answer questions written in a language you haven't learned yet.

We wrote a whole companion piece on this — what your practice exam score means — if you want to go deeper on reading your result.

Read your misses like an instructor would

When an instructor reviews a practice test, they don't count the misses — they sort them. Try sorting yours into three piles:

1. Didn't know it. You'd never seen the material. That's a study-plan item, not a failure — nobody is born knowing what ALARA stands for.

2. Knew it, second-guessed it. Your first instinct was right and you changed it. This pile shrinks with confidence, and confidence comes from repetition.

3. Misread the question. You knew the answer to the question they didn't ask. The fix is slowing down and re-reading the stem — especially words like "except," "first," and "most."

Ten minutes of sorting turns a discouraging number into a to-do list. And a to-do list is something you can actually work.

Turning one practice run into a study plan

Once you know your piles, the plan almost writes itself. Pile one becomes your reading list. Pile two becomes flashcard reps — our free dental terminology flashcards are built for exactly this. Pile three becomes a habit: on every future practice question, underline the operative word in the stem before you look at the answers. Then schedule a second practice run a week or two out and compare. The trend line matters far more than any single score.

Where this fits in real training

The practice exam is a mirror, not a teacher. It shows you where you stand; it can't put the knowledge in. That part happens in training — working through radiology, jurisprudence, and infection control properly, practicing chairside skills, and charting in the same kinds of tools real offices use. That's what our roughly 12-week program in Longview is built around, with classes in the daytime — call or text (903) 913-6444 for exact hours, or browse upcoming start dates. Prefer to start from home? The online program is self-paced and starts any day.

Either way, the practice exam stays free the whole time. Use it before you enroll to see where you're starting, and during training to watch the line go up. Watching your own score climb is one of the most motivating things in the entire program — because it's proof, in your own numbers, that you're becoming the person who can do this job.
