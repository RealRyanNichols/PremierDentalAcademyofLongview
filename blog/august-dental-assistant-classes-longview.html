MWF or Tue/Thu: how people actually choose

There's no wrong answer — the material is the same. The real question is which rhythm fits your life. The Mon/Wed/Fri cohort spreads the work across three shorter touches, which suits people who like frequent repetition and shorter gaps between classes. The Tue/Thu cohort concentrates it into two days, which tends to suit people juggling work shifts, childcare, or a longer drive — fewer trips to campus, more consolidated class time.

If you're commuting from Marshall, Kilgore, Henderson, Gladewater, or another East Texas town, the two-day pattern can save real windshield time over twelve weeks. We wrote more about weighing start dates in choosing your cohort — the logic applies to August just the same.

Why the class stays small

Each cohort is capped at 8 students. That's not a marketing flourish — it's how the training works. You learn chairside assisting by doing it with an instructor close enough to correct your hand position, not by watching from row six. Small classes also mean your instructor knows within the first week where you're strong and where you need another rep. The trade-off is honest: when 8 seats are spoken for, the next start date is the option.

What it costs

In-person tuition, plainly:

$3,000 paid in full, or $3,500 on a payment plan — $500 down holds your seat, then the $3,000 balance in simple weekly or monthly payments. No surprises, no fine print. The tuition planner lets you build your exact payment schedule with the same math as checkout.

Funding help is real, too: we work with Texas Workforce Solutions, WIOA, and GI Bill benefits. If money is the thing making you hesitate, read up on grants and financial aid options in Texas or ask us directly — we'd rather help you find a path than have you wait another year.
