Why we still answer the phone ourselves

There is one phone number on this website. It goes to Premier Dental Academy of Longview — one school, one campus on Gilmer Road. Not a sales floor, not a lead-routing service, not a chat bot wearing a name tag. We get asked why often enough that it is worth writing down.

The number on our website is a real phone

(903) 913-6444 is the school's line. You can call it or text it, and the person who picks it up or reads it is the person who can actually answer you — about the class schedule, about what a day looks like, about what happens if your car breaks down in week four.

That is not a technology achievement. It is a choice we keep making, and it costs us something. Answering the phone yourself means getting interrupted. It means the person who runs the school is also the person who explains, for the ninth time this month, what the difference is between finishing a course and registering with the state. We do it anyway, for a reason that has nothing to do with being nice.

Deciding to train for a new career is one of the few big decisions people make almost entirely on bad information. If the only way to get a straight answer is to fill out a form and wait to be called by someone reading from a script, most people never get the answer. They just quietly decide it is not for them.

What usually happens instead

Here is the pattern anyone who has shopped for training online will recognize. You find a program. The price is not on the site. You fill in your name, your email and your phone number to “learn more.” Then the calls start — from a number you do not know, from someone who does not teach there, who has a quota and a rebuttal ready for whatever you say.

Two different jobs

A call center's job is to move you to the next step. A school's job is to tell you the truth about whether the next step is a good idea for you. Those two jobs look identical for about ninety seconds, and then they stop looking anything alike.

We are not going to pretend we have no interest in filling a class. We obviously do. But a student who enrolls because nobody told them the hard parts does not finish, and a school our size cannot afford students who do not finish. Being straight with you on the phone is the cheaper option for us, not the noble one.

The questions people are most afraid to ask

The questions that stop people are almost never about curriculum. They are the ones that feel embarrassing to say out loud to a stranger. Tap any of these — these are the real ones, and none of them are a problem.

Why a text is often the better move

A lot of people would rather text than talk, and that is completely fine. Text the same number. Type the question the way you would say it to a friend, misspellings and all. You do not have to compose anything.

Texting has a practical advantage too: you get the answer in writing. If someone tells you a date, a schedule or a payment arrangement out loud, you are relying on your memory of a phone call. In a text thread, it is just there, and you can hand your phone to whoever is helping you decide.

If nobody picks up

Classes run in the daytime, so there are stretches when the person you want is standing in a room full of students with a handpiece in play. Leave a message or send a text and it gets read. You will not be handed off to a stranger in another state, and nobody is going to sell your number to anyone.

What we will and will not say on that call

Some of this is worth stating plainly so you know what you are getting.

We will tell you the real start dates, walk you through both ways to pay and what each one looks like, explain what the course does and does not include, and tell you honestly if we think a different path fits your life better than ours does — including the self-paced online course if driving to Longview two or three mornings a week is not realistic.

We will not promise you a job, a wage, a seat we have not confirmed, or an exam result. Finishing our course and being registered by the state are two separate things, and anyone who blurs that line on a sales call is doing you harm. We say it on the phone the same way we say it in writing.

If you would rather look than ask

Not everyone wants to start with a conversation, and you should not have to. A campus tour is free and there is nothing to commit to — you walk in, see the room, see the trainers we built, and ask whatever you came to ask with the equipment sitting right there in front of you. Plenty of people find that easier than a phone call.

If you want the background first, our story explains who runs this school and how, and the contact page has every way to reach us in one place. Start wherever is comfortable. The point is only that a real person is on the other end of all of it.
