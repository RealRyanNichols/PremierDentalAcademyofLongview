What is actually on the Texas RDA exam, topic by topic

When people ask what's on the exam, they're usually asking something else underneath: is there anything on there I've never heard of? Almost always, no. The subject areas are the same ones a good assistant uses on an ordinary Tuesday. Here's each one, and what studying it actually looks like.

Before the topics: where the official answers live

We're going to walk the subject matter. We are deliberately not going to tell you how many questions there are, how long you get, what score passes, what it costs, or what happens if you need to sit it again. Those details belong to the Texas State Board of Dental Examiners, they change, and a confident blog post quoting last year's version is worse than no post at all.

Check the format yourself, once

Before you start studying, spend ten minutes on tsbde.texas.gov and read the current requirements and exam information straight from the source. Ten minutes there is worth more than an hour of forum posts from people who took it in a different year.

The five subject areas — tap one to open it

Each card below opens with what the area covers and, more usefully, what actually studying it looks like. Nothing is saved and nothing is scored; this is just a way to read it in the order that helps you.

How to turn this into a study week

The mistake almost everyone makes is reading everything a little and nothing properly. A week that works looks more like this: pick the two areas you're weakest in and give them real, quiet time early in the week when your head is clear. Give the areas you're solid on a short review later in the week — not to learn them, just to keep them warm.

Then practice under something like real conditions at least once. Sitting down, phone away, no pausing to look things up. The gap between "I know this" and "I can produce this in a room with a clock running" is the entire game, and the only way to close it is to rehearse the second thing, not the first.

Your study order

    Where to actually practice

    Reading about an exam and rehearsing for one are different activities, and only the second one moves the needle. Our free Texas RDA practice exam is open to anyone — no enrollment, no payment — and gives you an explanation with every answer, which is the part that teaches. It's practice, not the official state exam, and it isn't affiliated with the Board.

    If you want structure rather than a question bank, the exam-prep mini-course walks the material in order, the study pack is the printable set for people who study better on paper, and Exam Pro is the deeper timed-practice option. For anyone who'd rather work through it with a person, we also do one-on-one state board prep. And if you just want something free tonight, the study guide and flashcards are both open.

    One honest thing about nerves

    Being nervous isn't evidence that you're unprepared — it's usually evidence that you care about the result. If it's genuinely keeping you from working, read how hard the exam really is and what happens if you don't pass the first time. Neither one is as final as it feels at 11 p.m.

    If you haven't trained yet

    All of the above assumes you've already learned the material somewhere. If you haven't, that's the actual first step, and the exam is a long way down the road. Our Longview program runs about twelve weeks with classes capped at eight students — you'll take real x-rays, set real trays, and chart in the same kind of practice-management software East Texas offices run. There's a fully online path with the same curriculum if the drive doesn't work for your life. Start with what the program covers, or use the tuition planner to see what the payments would look like.
