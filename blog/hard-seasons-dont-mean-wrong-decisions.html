A hard season doesn't mean you made the wrong decision

There's a moment after every big decision — usually around week three — when it gets hard, and a persuasive little voice says: see? You chose wrong. That voice is confusing two very different things. Hard is a season. Wrong is a direction. They require completely different responses, and mixing them up is how people abandon right paths at their steepest point.

Why right decisions feel wrong at first

Every real change front-loads its costs and back-loads its rewards. The new career pays its awkwardness, exhaustion, and identity wobble in the first weeks — and pays its competence, community, and paycheck later. So of COURSE week three feels like a mistake: you're standing at the point of maximum cost and minimum reward. That's not evidence of a wrong turn. That's the receipt for a real one. Comfortable choices don't have this phase, which is exactly what's wrong with them.

The question that separates hard from wrong

Ask it straight

"If I were already good at this, would I want it?" If yes — you're in a hard season on a right path; the problem is skill and time, both of which are coming. If honestly no — if even the mastered version of this life doesn't appeal — that's direction, and it deserves the wrong-path protocol, not more grinding. Most week-three panics answer 'yes' to this question. Most people never ask it.

How to get through the steep part

If the answer came back 'hard, not wrong': shrink the horizon. You don't have to finish the program today — you have to finish Tuesday. Stack small wins, use the feeling-behind playbook if it's school-shaped, and say the struggle out loud to one person — instructors included; in an eight-seat class we see the steep part happening and lean in, because we know exactly what week it is. The steep part has an end. It usually arrives right after the point where most people quit — which is the whole cruel joke of it, and the whole reason to stay.

For the person still deciding, scared of the hard part

Knowing the steep weeks are coming is an advantage — you can plan for them like weather. Line up your support before day one, put the question above somewhere you'll see it in week three, and remember: the people who finished weren't the ones who didn't struggle. They were the ones who read the struggle correctly.
