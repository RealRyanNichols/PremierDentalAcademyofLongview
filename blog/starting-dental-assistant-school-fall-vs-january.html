Start this fall or wait for January?

Every year around now, people call and say some version of the same thing: I want to do this, but maybe I should just start after the holidays. It is a reasonable instinct and sometimes it is the right call. It is also, for a lot of people, the sentence that quietly costs them a year. Here is the honest version of both sides, with no urgency tactics attached.

First, the dates that actually exist

Before we compare anything, here is what is really on the calendar. Five in-person starts remain in 2026 on the Longview campus: September 29, October 5, October 20, November 9 and November 17. Classes are daytime — Mon/Wed/Fri run 8:30 AM – 12:30 PM and Tue/Thu run 9:00 AM – 3:00 PM, depending on the cohort. There are no evening, night or Saturday classes.

And the part that matters for this whole article: there are no January dates posted on our calendar right now. That is not a scarcity trick — it means they have not been scheduled yet. If you are planning around January, call or text (903) 913-6444 and ask where that stands. When dates get set, they appear on the calendar first.

So "wait for January" is, at this moment, a decision to wait for a date nobody can hand you yet. That does not make it wrong. It does make it worth saying out loud.

What twelve weeks does to a holiday calendar

The program runs about twelve weeks. Lay that against the rest of the year and the shape of each option gets clear fast. An early-October start puts your last weeks near the end of December. A mid-November start carries you into February. Those are approximate — holiday weeks shift the schedule, so ask for the exact end date of whichever cohort you are looking at rather than counting squares on your own calendar.

What you are really choosing between is which twelve weeks of your life get the hard part. Starting in October means Thanksgiving and the December stretch land inside your program. Starting later means the busy season is behind you and the program is in front of you. Neither of those is automatically easier. They are just hard in different places.

The case for starting this fall

The strongest argument for now is not urgency. It is momentum. People who decide in September and start in October are still carrying the energy of the decision. People who decide in September and start in January have to carry that decision through four months of ordinary life, a holiday season, and every reason to put it down. Some do. A lot do not, and they are not weak — they are just human, and four months is a long time to hold something you are nervous about.

The practical arguments follow from that. You are working through the material while your reasons are still loud. You finish sooner, which means you are looking for work sooner. And a fall start means the holiday weeks arrive when you already know your classmates and your instructor, which is a genuinely easier time to be tired than week one is.

One thing we will not tell you: that seats are about to vanish, or that this price is about to change, or that this is your last chance at anything. Classes cap at eight because that is how the room is designed, not as a countdown clock. If someone is selling you a decision with a timer on it, that is worth noticing.

The honest case for waiting

Now the other side, and we mean it. There are people who should wait, and telling them otherwise would be doing them harm to make a sale.

Wait if the money is not settled. Twelve weeks is short, but it is twelve weeks of tuition, gas, and possibly fewer hours at work — and starting a program you have to stop halfway through is worse than starting it later. Run your own numbers in the tuition planner and read the current figures on the enrollment page; nothing about tuition is typed into this article, on purpose, so an old page can never quote you a stale number.

Wait if childcare for daytime hours is not actually arranged — not "my sister probably can," but arranged. Wait if you are three weeks from a move, a baby, a surgery, or the end of a lease. Wait if your current job has a season that owns November and December and you already know how that goes. In every one of those cases, waiting is not procrastination. It is planning, and it is the reason you will finish.

If you wait, wait on purpose

Waiting fails when it is vague. "After the holidays" is not a plan; it is a feeling, and feelings do not put you in a classroom. If you are going to wait, give it edges: a date you will call to ask what is scheduled, a number you need saved by, a conversation you need to have with your employer or your family, and a person who knows you said it.

The other thing worth doing while you wait is not waiting quietly. Nothing stops you from learning the vocabulary, getting comfortable with charting, or finding out whether the work actually suits you before you spend a dollar. Try Practice Pro or the ChairSide trainer on your phone tonight. If you hate it, you have learned something valuable for free. If you love it, January gets easier to wait for — and easier to start.

Funding is the other thing to work on during a wait rather than after it. Texas Workforce Solutions, WIOA and veterans benefits all have their own timelines and their own eligibility rules, and those are decided by the agency, not by us. The funding finder will point you at who to call. Start those conversations early; they are slower than you expect.

The option that ignores the calendar entirely

There is a third answer people forget to consider, because the question is usually framed as now-or-January. The online program is self-paced and starts any day — including today, including the week of Christmas, including whenever your life settles down.

Be clear-eyed about what it is, though. It is coursework and software practice, not physical hands-on training in a room with equipment and an instructor, and we would rather say that plainly than blur the two. For some people it is exactly right; for others it is a way to keep moving now and come to the campus later. The full comparison is here — read it before you choose, not after.

What to do this week

You do not have to decide today, and you should not decide from a webpage. Do these three things instead. One: look at the real dates on the calendar and pick the one that fits your week, not the one that fits your mood. Two: come see the room — a tour is free, takes an ordinary weekday, and answers more in twenty minutes than a month of reading. Three: if the money question is the real question, start the funding conversation now regardless of which start date you choose.

Then decide. Starting in October is a fine answer. Waiting until the new year, with a date and a plan attached, is also a fine answer. The only answer that costs you anything is the one where you never actually decide — and that one is the most common of all.

If you want to talk it through with a person rather than a page, call or text (903) 913-6444. We will tell you to wait if waiting is right. It happens more often than you would think.
