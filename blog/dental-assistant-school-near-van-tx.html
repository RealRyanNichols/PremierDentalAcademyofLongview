Start with the geography, not the sales pitch

From Van to our campus in Longview is roughly an hour and a quarter, depending on your route and where in the county you start — and that is an estimate, so map your own address before you weigh anything else on this page. Tyler sits between us and you, which means there are almost certainly options closer to your kitchen than we are.

We are not going to spend this page telling you what is wrong with anyone nearer. We do not know those rooms, and a school that builds its case out of other schools' faults has told you it does not have much of a case. What we can do is describe ours precisely enough that you can judge for yourself whether it is worth passing anything to get here.

The honest test for 'is farther worth it'

Distance is only ever worth it when the thing at the end of it is different in a way you can name. Not nicer. Different. If two programs teach the same material in rooms that work the same way, the closer one wins and it is not close.

So ask any school you are looking at — us included — three things you can actually check: how many people are in the room, what equipment your hands touch, and who is standing there teaching. If the answers come back the same everywhere, drive the shorter distance. Our answers to those three are below.

One: how many people are in the room

Our classes are capped at eight. That number is the whole design, not a detail — it decides how often you are the person holding the instrument instead of the person watching someone else hold it. In a small room you cannot hide in the back, which is uncomfortable for about a week and then becomes the reason you actually learn.

What a class of eight feels like goes into what that is like day to day. Ask every school you visit for their number. A school that answers with a range, or with the word "small," has not answered.

Two: what your hands actually touch

The room here is set up to work like a dental office rather than like a lecture hall — real equipment and instruments, arranged the way they sit in practice. Alongside that we built our own software trainers, because the part new assistants struggle with is rarely the clinical work; it is the charting and the paperwork nobody practices until their first real job.

So there is a tooth chart, a six-point AAP perio chart, a prescription pad with an allergy guard, a drag-and-drop schedule, SOAP-note documentation, and the insurance claim and EOB workflow. You can look at two of them right now without enrolling: Practice Pro and the ChairSide trainer. Judge them yourself — that is more useful than us describing them.

This is the part that is genuinely hard to find elsewhere, and it is the honest reason some people decide the drive is worth it. It is also the part you can evaluate from your couch in Van before you spend a single tank of gas.

Three: who is teaching

Amanda Williams owns this school and teaches in it. When you ask a question in week six, you are asking the person whose name is on the building. That is not automatically better than a larger program with a teaching staff — but it is different, it is checkable, and it is a fair thing to weigh.

Ask anywhere you are considering: who will be in the room with me, every class day, by name? Then see how long the answer takes.

The schedule, the online option, and the money

In-person classes are daytime, in one of two formats: Mon/Wed/Fri, 8:30 AM – 12:30 PM or Tue/Thu, 9:00 AM – 3:00 PM, depending on the cohort. There are no evening, night or Saturday classes. At your distance, count trips rather than hours — three shorter mornings a week is more highway than two longer days, and over twelve weeks that gap is what decides whether people finish.

If the drive does not survive that math, the online program is self-paced and starts any day, with no commute at all. It is coursework and software practice rather than physical hands-on training, and we would rather say that plainly than sell it as the same thing — here is the full comparison.

Texas Workforce Solutions, WIOA and veterans benefits are worth asking about — eligibility is theirs to decide, not ours, so call your local office and ask directly.

We hold TWC Career School License #S5316, which you can confirm through the Texas Workforce Commission rather than taking our word for it, and state registration as a dental assistant is a separate process with its own current requirements at tsbde.texas.gov.
