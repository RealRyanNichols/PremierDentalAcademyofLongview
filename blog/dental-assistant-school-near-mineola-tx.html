The schedule question comes before the school question

Mineola to our campus on Gilmer Road in Longview is roughly an hour, give or take, depending on your road and where you are starting from — map your own address, because that is the only number your alarm clock will care about.

Here is what people get wrong: they choose the school first and then try to bolt a schedule onto their life. Do it the other way around. Our in-person classes are daytime, in one of two formats, and the approved hours are Mon/Wed/Fri, 8:30 AM – 12:30 PM or Tue/Thu, 9:00 AM – 3:00 PM depending on the class. There are no evening, night or Saturday classes.

At an hour out, count trips, not hours. Mon/Wed/Fri is a shorter morning but three round trips a week. Tue/Thu is a longer day but only two. Over twelve weeks that difference is not small, and it is usually the thing that decides whether somebody finishes.

What that means if you are working

Daytime classes and a full-time day job do not overlap gracefully, and we are not going to draw you a diagram pretending they do. Some people make it work by shifting to evenings or weekends at work, some use vacation, some change jobs for twelve weeks, and some decide the timing is wrong this quarter and come back in the spring. All four of those are reasonable. What is not reasonable is signing up for something you already know your schedule cannot hold, which is why it is worth saying out loud now rather than in week three.

If your job is the immovable object, that is exactly the situation the online program exists for. It is self-paced and starts any day.

What online covers, and what it does not

The online program runs the same curriculum on your own clock, from home, with no drive. That is a genuine advantage at your distance and we will not undersell it.

What it is not is physical hands-on training. You are not holding instruments, and nobody is correcting your grip in real time. That is a real difference, not a marketing asterisk — and any school that tells you their online course is identical to being in a room with equipment has just told you what kind of school it is. Online vs. in-person training walks through the trade in more detail.

A decent way to decide: picture yourself six weeks in, tired, on a Tuesday. Which version of this do you still show up for — the one that needs an hour of highway, or the one that needs you to open a laptop after the house goes quiet? Be honest. The answer is usually obvious and usually ignored.

Closer is not automatically better, and neither are we

There are programs nearer to Mineola than we are. We are not going to say a word against any of them — we do not know your life, and a school that spends its energy running down the competition is telling on itself.

What we will say is that proximity is one variable out of about seven, and it is the easiest one to measure, which is why people over-weight it. Class size, who is actually teaching, what equipment you get your hands on, what the credential legally is, whether you can visit before paying — those are harder to check and they matter more. So check them, on us and on everyone else.

Verify before you pay — including us

Premier Dental Academy of Longview holds TWC Career School License #S5316. Do not take that from a web page; confirm it yourself through the Texas Workforce Commission, and do the same for any school you are considering.

Be equally clear on what finishing gets you. A school issues a certificate of completion. Registration as a dental assistant in Texas is a separate process run by the state, with its own requirements that change over time — they live at tsbde.texas.gov, and that is where you should read them rather than trusting anyone's summary, ours included.
