The honest starting point

Henderson doesn't have a dedicated dental assisting school inside the city limits — most Rusk County folks who want this career end up training in a bigger East Texas hub. For Henderson, the closest practical option is Longview, straight up US-259. That's where our campus sits: 2800 Gilmer Rd, Suite 106, Longview, TX.

So the real question isn't "is there a school in Henderson?" It's "is the drive worth it?" We think the honest answer is yes — and not because we're the ones saying it, but because of how the schedule works.

The drive, without sugarcoating

Henderson to our Longview campus is roughly 25 miles up US-259 — most people make it in about half an hour, door to door. That would be a real grind five days a week. But dental assistant training here isn't five days a week: classes meet two or three days a week (our cohorts run Monday/Wednesday/Friday or Tuesday/Thursday), during the daytime, for about 12 weeks. Then you're done.

What training in Longview actually looks like

This isn't a lecture hall. Classes are capped at 8 students, taught by our founder Amanda Williams, RDA, and built to feel like a working dental office from day one — real instruments, real tray setups, and the same practice-management and clinical software workflows you'll use on the job, from charting on an anatomical tooth chart to writing SOAP notes and working insurance claims in our own trainers. By the time you walk into a Henderson or Longview office for an interview, the operatory won't be new to you.

Daytime classes only

All in-person cohorts meet during the day. For exact class hours, call or text (903) 913-6444 — it's Amanda who answers, not a call center.

What it costs — and how Henderson students pay for it

In-person tuition is $3,000 paid in full, or $3,500 on a payment plan: $500 down holds your seat, then the $3,000 balance in simple weekly or monthly payments. No surprise fees bolted on later. If you want to see exactly what a plan would look like for your budget, the free Tuition Planner runs the same math as our checkout.

You may not have to pay out of pocket

We accept Texas Workforce Solutions funding, WIOA, and the GI Bill. Rusk County residents can start that conversation with their local Workforce Solutions office — or just ask us and we'll point you in the right direction.

The small-town advantage nobody talks about

Here's something we've written about before: small-town dental offices and new RDAs are a genuinely good match. Offices in towns like Henderson, Overton, and Tatum don't get a stack of applications every week the way big-city practices do. When a trained, certified assistant who already lives in town shows up, that's not one resume in a pile — that's often the answer to a staffing problem the office has had for months.

Train in Longview for 12 weeks, and you can come home to work. That's the whole play.
