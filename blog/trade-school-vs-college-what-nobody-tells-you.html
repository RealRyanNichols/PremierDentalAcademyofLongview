Trade school vs. college: what nobody tells you at 18

Everyone over 30 has an opinion about what you should do after graduation, and almost none of them will show you the actual trade-offs side by side. So here they are — college, trade school, straight to work, military — compared honestly by a school that only wins if you pick one specific path, which is exactly why we're going to be careful not to oversell it.

The part nobody says out loud

The four paths are not ranked. They are trade-offs across three currencies: time (how long before you earn), money (what it costs and what debt follows you), and certainty (how sure you are about what you want). Adults push college because it kept the most doors open in their economy. Adults push trades because degrees got expensive in yours. Both are pattern-matching their own lives — your job is to match the path to your actual situation, not to their memories.

The question that sorts it

Ask yourself this, honestly

Do you know what you want to do, or do you know how you like to work? If you know the what (nursing, engineering, teaching), pick the path that credential requires — sometimes that's college, full stop. If you only know the how ("with my hands," "with people," "not at a desk"), a short credential that gets you working in a real field beats four expensive years of undecided. You can layer degrees on later; plenty of people do it in reverse.
