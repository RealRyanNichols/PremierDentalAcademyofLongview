Burnout or wrong path? How to tell the difference before you quit

Exhaustion is a terrible advisor. From inside a depleted season, 'I need a nap' and 'I need a new life' feel exactly the same — and people regularly quit careers that only needed a vacation, or take vacations from careers that needed quitting. Before any big move, run the distinction. The cures are opposites.

The core distinction

Burnout is depletion in a right place. The work itself still means something when you can see straight — you're just running on empty from pace, understaffing, a brutal season. Wrong path is fullness in a wrong place. You could be perfectly rested and the work would still feel like wearing someone else's clothes. The tell is what a good day does: after a real win at work, burnout feels a flicker of the old satisfaction — wrong-path feels nothing, or feels 'fine, but so what.' Track your good days for two weeks and the pattern speaks.

What NOT to do while depleted

The one rule of exhausted decisions

Don't make irreversible moves from the bottom of the tank. Quit-texts, bridge-burnings, signing up for anything expensive at midnight — all of it waits until you've had two decent weeks. (Reversible research — reading, quizzes, one tour — is fine and actually helps, because data soothes spirals.) The decision method has the full protocol.

If the answer comes back 'wrong path'

Then rest alone won't fix it, and the kindest thing you can do for future-you is start the change small and concrete: the map of fast credentials, the free fit quiz if healthcare pulls at you, stories from people who made the jump — from retail, from CNA work, from everywhere else. Twelve weeks is a small door for how big the room behind it is.

If it's deeper than either

Sometimes the gray isn't the job at all. If sleep, appetite, and joy have been gone for weeks regardless of work — that's a conversation for a doctor or counselor, not a career blog, and having it is strength, not defeat. The path questions will still be here after, and they'll be easier to answer with the lights back on.
