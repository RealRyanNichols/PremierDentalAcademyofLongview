Texas dental assistant certifications: the complete checklist

"Certification" is the single most confusing word in this career. People use it to mean six different things — a school certificate, state registration, an X-ray credential, a CPR card, an add-on permit. Here is every piece laid out in plain English, in the order most people actually collect them.

First, untangle the three words

Almost every mix-up in this career traces back to three words that sound the same and mean different things.

A certificate is what a school gives you when you finish its program. That is us. It proves you completed training. Registration is what the state gives you — in Texas, the Registered Dental Assistant (RDA) credential comes from the Texas State Board of Dental Examiners. Certification in everyday conversation usually means one of the specific add-on credentials below, like radiology or CPR.

Finishing school and being registered by the state are two separate milestones. We are proud of the first and we prepare you hard for the second — but no school anywhere can hand you the state's credential. If somebody tells you otherwise, walk away. We wrote a whole post on how to verify a Texas dental assistant school for exactly this reason.

Rules change. Always verify at the source.

Requirements, fees, timelines, and renewal cycles for every credential on this page are set by the state — not by us — and they get updated. Use this checklist to know what to look for, then confirm the current details directly at tsbde.texas.gov before you pay for anything.

1 & 2. Training, then RDA registration

The training program is the foundation. Ours runs about 12 weeks in person at the Longview campus, capped at eight students so nobody hides in the back — classes are daytime, and you can call or text (903) 913-6444 for exact hours or check upcoming start dates. The next cohorts start August 17 and August 25, 2026, with September 14 and September 29 after that.

State registration is the milestone that lets you legally work as an RDA in Texas and it is what most job postings mean when they say "certified." The application, the eligibility requirements, and the exam pieces all live with TSBDE. Our Texas RDA registration guide walks the process step by step — it is the most-read article on this site, which tells you how many people are stuck on this exact question.

3. Dental radiology — the one on every job post

X-rays are a daily part of the job, and taking them is a regulated skill in Texas. This is the credential we get asked about more than any other single add-on, and it is the one hiring offices scan résumés for. It is also very learnable: positioning, technique, and the safety habits that come before technique.

Start with how to get dental radiology certification in Texas, then build the underlying skill with X-ray safety basics and periapical vs. bitewing. When it is time to test, the radiography portion of the exam has its own study approach.

4 & 5. Jurisprudence and CPR/BLS

Jurisprudence is the rules-of-the-road piece: what Texas law and board rules say a dental assistant may and may not do. It is not clinical, it is not scary, and it rewards reading carefully. Our jurisprudence exam guide covers how to prepare without cramming.

CPR/BLS is the one credential on this list with an expiration date you will be renewing for the rest of your career. Offices want a current card on file, and providers around East Texas run classes regularly. See CPR and BLS certification for dental assistants for what to look for in a course.

A word about "stackable" credentials

You do not need all six on day one. Get registered, get working, then add credentials as your office needs them — several offices will happily point you toward the next one because it makes you more useful to them. Career direction, honestly discussed: how a dental assistant career advances.

6. The add-ons: coronal polishing and nitrous monitoring

These two are optional permits that expand what you are allowed to do chairside, and they tend to make you more valuable to a practice. Coronal polishing certification in Texas and nitrous oxide monitoring certification each have their own coursework and board requirements — verify both at TSBDE before enrolling in any add-on course.

Not every office uses both. Ask in your interview which ones the practice actually wants; that answer is worth more than a guess. Our interview questions and answers post has a good list of questions to ask them.

Where the money comes from

Tuition here is $3,000 paid in full, or $3,500 on a plan — $500 down holds your seat and the $3,000 balance goes on simple weekly or monthly payments. Online is $997, self-paced, and if you start online and move to in-person within 90 days that $997 transfers in full as credit.

Funding routes are real and worth a phone call before you pay out of pocket: Workforce Solutions East Texas, WIOA, and GI Bill benefits are all used by students here. Start with the funding finder, then read how WIOA funding works in East Texas and the Workforce Solutions voucher process. For pay expectations, see our salary page rather than any number you read on a forum.
