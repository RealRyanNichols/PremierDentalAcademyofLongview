Let us be honest about the drive

Athens is in Henderson County, out past Tyler, and our campus is at 2800 Gilmer Rd, Suite 106, in Longview. That is somewhere around an hour and twenty minutes each way on a good day.

Three class days a week at that distance is roughly eight hours of driving before you have learned anything. Some people do things like that and are fine. Most people, honestly, burn out on it around week four — and a program you quit in week four has cost you money and time and given you nothing back. We would rather say that up front than sell you a seat you will not sit in.

So start with the online program

For most people in Athens, the online program is the realistic answer. It is self-paced, it starts whatever day you decide to start, and it covers the same curriculum we teach on campus. You do it on your phone or a laptop, in whatever pieces your week allows.

Here is what it is not, said plainly: it is not physical hands-on training. Nobody learns to seat a patient, pass an instrument, or take a radiograph through a screen, and any school telling you otherwise is doing you harm. What online gives you is the knowledge half — terminology, anatomy, infection control, radiography theory, charting, the office workflow — which is a large share of what you need and all of what you can honestly learn remotely.

The practical way people use it: get the knowledge in place from home, then arrange the hands-on piece however your situation allows. This comparison lays out both sides without a sales pitch.

A short program is not a lesser choice

Athens is a college town, and if you have grown up around one you have probably absorbed the idea that a real education takes four years. Plenty of good careers do. This one does not, and pretending otherwise would not make it truer.

Dental assisting is a hands-and-judgment job. What an office needs to see is that you can chart accurately, keep a sterile field honestly, hold a schedule together, and stay calm with a nervous patient. Those are trained skills, not degree skills. Our program runs about 12 weeks because that is roughly how long it takes to build them to a working standard — not because we cut corners off something longer.

What matters more than program length is whether the school is licensed and whether you can verify it. We hold TWC Career School License #S5316, and you can look that up yourself. We wrote how to verify any Texas school for exactly that reason — use it on us too.

If you are weighing a short program against a longer one, this breakdown and this one on program length cover the trade-offs without knocking anybody.

When the campus is worth the miles

Some people should make the drive, and we are not going to talk them out of it. It is worth it when you learn by doing and know that about yourself, when you are already looking at Tyler or Longview for work, or when you want the accountability of a room with seven other people and an instructor who notices you are struggling before you say so.

Classes are capped at eight. That is the whole reason the drive can pay for itself — you are not commuting to a lecture you could have streamed. You are commuting to chair time, an instructor's attention, and equipment you cannot put in your living room.

If that is you, the schedule choice matters. Classes are daytime, in two patterns:

  • Mon/Wed/Fri — 8:30 AM to 12:30 PM. Three mornings a week.
  • Tue/Thu — 9:00 AM to 3:00 PM. Two longer days.

From Athens, the Tue/Thu pattern is the one that is even arguable. Two round trips a week is a commitment; three is a second job. Those two lines are the only class hours we publish. If you find any after-hours times attached to our name somewhere, that listing is out of date.

What both paths have in common

Whichever way you go, you get the same curriculum and the same practice-management software we built for our own students. Practice Pro has an anatomical tooth chart, a six-point periodontal chart, a prescription pad with an allergy guard, a drag-and-drop schedule, SmartDoc clinical notes, and an ADA claims and EOB workflow — the office half of the job that most programs skip entirely.

You can open the trainer, the flashcards, and a practice exam right now, from Athens, for free, without giving us a dollar. That is the fastest way to find out whether this material interests you at all.

How to decide

You do not need to resolve this in one sitting. Pick whichever of these sounds most like your actual life and read that one.

Paying for either one

Eligibility is theirs to decide, not ours, but people talk themselves out of that phone call all the time and it costs nothing to make. The funding finder tells you which doors to try.

One thing to be clear about

Completing our program earns a Premier Dental Academy Certificate of Completion. Registering as a Registered Dental Assistant in Texas is a separate process run by the state, with its own requirements, fees, and timelines that are theirs to set. For anything official, go straight to tsbde.texas.gov. Our registration overview explains how the pieces fit together, and what a school can and cannot do for you.
