Dental Charting Abbreviations

The shorthand you'll read and write on charts, routing slips and the schedule every day. Learn what each one means here, then drill them until they're automatic in the flashcards.

Surfaces & directional

Where on the tooth — used together (e.g. "MOD") to describe a cavity or filling.

M
Mesial
Toward the midline of the arch.
D
Distal
Away from the midline of the arch.
I
Incisal
The biting edge of a front tooth.
O
Occlusal
The chewing surface of a back tooth.
F
Facial
Toward the lips/cheek (outer side).
B
Buccal
Toward the cheek (facial side of back teeth).
L
Lingual
Toward the tongue (inner side).
P
Palatal
Toward the palate — the word many offices use for the lingual surface of an upper tooth.
MO
Mesio-occlusal
A two-surface cavity or filling involving the mesial and the occlusal.
DO
Disto-occlusal
A two-surface cavity or filling involving the distal and the occlusal.
MOD
Mesio-occluso-distal
A three-surface restoration that crosses the mesial, occlusal and distal.
Prox
Proximal
The surface facing the neighboring tooth — mesial or distal.
Cerv
Cervical
The neck of the tooth, where the crown meets the root near the gumline.
🔲

Quadrants & areas

The four sections of the mouth, viewed from the patient.

Quad
Quadrant
One of the four sections of the mouth.
UR
Upper Right
Maxillary right quadrant.
UL
Upper Left
Maxillary left quadrant.
LL
Lower Left
Mandibular left quadrant.
LR
Lower Right
Mandibular right quadrant.
Max
Maxillary
The upper jaw and the upper arch of teeth.
Mand
Mandibular
The lower jaw and the lower arch of teeth.
Ant
Anterior
The front teeth — incisors and canines.
Post
Posterior
The back teeth — premolars and molars.
🛠️

Materials & restorations

What the tooth is restored with — fillings, crowns and more.

C / COMP
Composite
Tooth-colored resin filling.
AM / AMAL
Amalgam
Silver-colored metal filling.
CR/BR
Crown & Bridge
Cap on a tooth (crown) or span across a gap (bridge).
FGC
Full Gold Crown
Crown made entirely of gold alloy.
PFM
Porcelain Fused to Metal
Metal substructure under tooth-colored porcelain.
PVC
Porcelain Veneer Crown
Crown faced with porcelain for esthetics.
SSC
Stainless Steel Crown
Prefab metal crown, often used on baby teeth.
BU
Buildup
Material added to rebuild a tooth to support a crown.
PNC
Post and Core
Post in the canal plus a core to retain a crown.
S
Sealant
Protective coating in the pits and grooves.
GI / GIC
Glass Ionomer
A fluoride-releasing material used for fillings, liners and cementing.
IRM
Intermediate Restorative Material
A reinforced zinc oxide–eugenol material mixed chairside for temporary fillings.
TEMP
Temporary
A short-term crown or filling worn while the final restoration is made.
RPD
Removable Partial Denture
Replaces some missing teeth in an arch; the patient takes it out to clean it.
CD
Complete Denture
Replaces all of the teeth in one arch.
🪥

Procedures

What was done — or what is planned.

RCT
Root Canal Treatment
Removes infected pulp and fills the canal.
SRP
Scaling and Root Planing
Deep cleaning below the gumline.
EXT
Extraction
Removal of a tooth.
PX
Prophy (prophylaxis)
A routine cleaning.
FL / FV
Fluoride / Fluoride Varnish
Topical fluoride treatment; FV is the varnish form painted on the teeth.
IMP
Impression
A mold of the teeth used to make models or appliances.
TX
Treatment
Care provided to the patient (also "Tx").
ENDO
Endodontics
The specialty and procedures that treat the pulp and root canals.
OS
Oral Surgery
Surgical care such as extractions, biopsies and implant placement.
ORTHO
Orthodontics
Braces and aligners that move teeth into position.
RECEM
Recement
Re-cementing a crown, bridge or band that has come loose.
ADJ
Adjustment
Reshaping a restoration, denture or bite so it fits and functions comfortably.
⚠️

Conditions & diagnoses

What's wrong — chief complaints and findings.

TA
Toothache
Pain in or around a tooth.
DS
Dry Socket
Lost blood clot after an extraction (alveolar osteitis).
ABS
Abscess
A localized pocket of pus from infection.
V
Class V (five)
A cavity in the gingival third of the facial or lingual surface of any tooth.
CC
Chief Complaint
Why the patient says they came in, recorded in the patient's own words.
DX
Diagnosis
The provider's conclusion about what the problem is (also written "Dx").
FX
Fracture
A broken or cracked tooth or restoration.
PARL
Periapical Radiolucency
A dark area at the root tip on a radiograph that can signal infection.
TMJ
Temporomandibular Joint
The jaw joint; pain or dysfunction there is usually charted as TMD.
BRUX
Bruxism
Clenching or grinding, often noted alongside worn or flattened surfaces.
🩻

Radiographs (x-rays)

The image types you take and chart.

FMX
Full Mouth Series
A complete set of x-rays of all teeth.
BWX / BW
Bitewing X-ray
Checks for decay between the back teeth.
PANO
Panoramic X-ray
One wide image of the entire upper and lower jaws.
PA
Periapical X-ray
Shows a whole tooth from the crown to the root tip plus the surrounding bone.
OCC
Occlusal X-ray
A larger image that captures a broad section of one arch at a time.
CBCT
Cone Beam Computed Tomography
A 3-D scan used for implant planning, impactions and surgery.
CEPH
Cephalometric X-ray
A side view of the head and jaws, used mostly in orthodontics.

Scheduling & admin

Front-desk and schedule shorthand.

NP
New Patient
First visit to the practice.
RS
Reschedule
Move the appointment to another time.
CA
Cancel
A canceled appointment.
BA
Broken Appointment
Missed or canceled on short notice.
NS
No-Show
Patient did not arrive.
RC
Recall / Recare
A returning patient due for a routine exam and cleaning.
ASAP
As Soon As Possible
On the short-call list — the patient will come in early if a slot opens.
EOB
Explanation of Benefits
The insurance statement showing what the plan paid and what the patient owes.
Pre-D
Predetermination
A request for the insurance plan's written estimate before treatment starts.
🩸

Periodontal

The gum and bone measurements you record during a perio charting.

Perio
Periodontal / Periodontics
The gums and supporting bone — and the specialty that treats them.
PD
Probing Depth
The millimeter reading from the gum margin to the base of the sulcus or pocket (also called pocket depth).
CAL
Clinical Attachment Level
Depth measured from the CEJ instead of the gum margin, so it reflects true attachment loss.
CEJ
Cementoenamel Junction
Where the enamel of the crown meets the cementum of the root — the landmark for attachment readings.
BOP
Bleeding on Probing
Bleeding when the site is probed — a sign of active inflammation.
REC
Recession
Gum tissue that has moved apically, leaving root surface exposed.
MOB
Mobility
How much a tooth moves when tested; recorded by class in the chart.
FURC
Furcation
Where the roots of a multi-rooted tooth divide; bone loss there is graded by class.
MGJ
Mucogingival Junction
The line where firm attached gingiva meets the looser alveolar mucosa.
💉

Anesthesia & sedation

What the provider is asking you to set up — and what it numbs.

N2O
Nitrous Oxide
Inhalation conscious sedation ("laughing gas").
LA
Local Anesthetic
Numbing medication injected to block pain in one area while the patient stays awake.
IANB
Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block
Numbs the lower teeth on one side, along with that side of the lower lip and chin.
PSA
Posterior Superior Alveolar (block)
Numbs the upper molars on one side; the mesiobuccal root of the first molar sometimes needs a second injection.
Infil
Infiltration
Anesthetic deposited near the root tip, used most often on the upper arch.
Epi
Epinephrine
A vasoconstrictor added to some anesthetics to extend the numbness and reduce bleeding.
📝

Charting status & symbols

The state of a tooth or finding in the record — check your own office key before you chart.

TP
Treatment Plan(ned)
Work that has been proposed and charted but not done yet.
EXIST
Existing
A restoration already in the mouth when the patient was first charted here.
MISS
Missing
The tooth is not present; many offices mark it with an X or a line through the tooth.
WATCH
Watch
A finding being monitored at each visit rather than treated now.
WNL
Within Normal Limits
Charted when an exam finding shows nothing abnormal.
NKDA
No Known Drug Allergies
Recorded on the health history when the patient reports no drug allergies.
